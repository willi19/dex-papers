DexMachina: Functional Retargeting for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation

ICML 2026 (arXiv 2505.24853, May 2025)Stanford University, NVIDIARLbimanualdexteroushuman-video
TL;DR

DexMachina uses a temporary virtual controller to move the object along a human demonstration, then gradually removes that help until the robot hands perform the task on their own.

Problem

  • Input: one tracked human hand–object demonstration.
  • Goal: reproduce the object's pose and articulation trajectory.
  • Target regime: long-horizon, bimanual, articulated-object tasks.

Why prior approaches fail

  • Kinematic retargeting copies hand motion but does not guarantee functional contact.
  • Object-state reward says what to achieve, but gives weak exploration guidance.
  • Long contact sequences fail early, so the policy rarely observes later subtasks.

Training recipe

  1. Retarget the human motion to obtain reference wrist motion, hand keypoints, and approximate contacts.
  2. Train PPO with object tracking, motion imitation, joint-reference, and contact rewards.
  3. Use virtual spring-damper controllers to keep the object on the demonstrated trajectory.
  4. Decay controller strength once the policy's rewards are stable; the learned policy takes over the physics.

What is actually new

  • The curriculum relaxes object dynamics, not just reward thresholds or simulator parameters.
  • The target is object function, so robot motion may differ from the human strategy.
  • The same setup evaluates algorithms and hand hardware without hand-specific method changes.

Evidence

  • 6 dexterous hands, 5 articulated objects, and 7 ARCTIC demonstrations.
  • Largest gains appear on long-horizon tasks where task and auxiliary rewards alone fail.
  • Kinematic replay may look correct while barely manipulating the object.

Takeaway

  • The distinctive ingredient is the decaying object controller. PPO and the reward terms are mostly familiar.
  • DexMachina turns one human trajectory into a task-specific RL curriculum and a functional test for different hand designs.
  • Scope: state-based policies trained and evaluated in simulation; this is not one generalist policy shared across every task and hand.
